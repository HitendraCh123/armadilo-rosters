Why Austin Businesses Choose Small-Batch Wholesale Coffee

If you’ve sourced coffee from a national distributor before, you already know what the alternative looks like: 500-pound automated roast cycles, a rotating product SKU list, bags that sat in a warehouse before they landed on your loading dock. The freshness window is a suggestion. The consistency is close, most of the time. That’s not how Mary and Andy operate. Here’s what 20–30 lb small-batch roasting actually means for your business:

Industrial wholesale roasters run 500+ pound automated batches because volume is their business model. Armadillo roasts 20–30 lbs at a time because quality is theirs. That’s not a marketing position — it’s a roasting constraint they’ve chosen deliberately:

  • Temperature precision within 2°F across the roast curve — small batch size means every bean in the drum experiences the same thermal environment
  • Immediate cooling and packaging after roasting — no holding time, no extended resting in bins waiting for the next distribution run
  • Roast profile consistency maintained across every batch — when lot characteristics shift, Mary and Andy adjust manually, not algorithmically
  • Lower roast defect rates than large commercial batches where edge beans and core beans develop differently

Every bag is labeled with the roast date so cafés and restaurants know exactly how fresh their coffee is.

Work directly with the roasters — not a distributor — for guidance, consistency, and custom blends.
